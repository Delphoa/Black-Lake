## Executive Summary

The selected DEP is a source-first snapshot of ten developments spanning agent safety, medical imaging, AI4Science, robotics, tool use, formal mathematics, cryptography, and energy research. The strongest cross-source result is methodological rather than a single model score: evaluation becomes more informative when it preserves interaction history, workflow stage, domain or population context, and an explicit verification substrate. TSJ reports that short-horizon companion tests can underestimate delayed developmental risk; BenchX reports subgroup and protocol weaknesses hidden by aggregate imaging metrics; AutoMedBench reports that validation and submission failures dominate its tagged workflow errors; and StateGen, LEAP, ASYS, and AlphaEvolve each place structured state, compiler feedback, mathematical constraints, or correctness/security tests inside the loop.

These are source-reported findings, not independently reproduced results. The reviewer interpretation is that the source set supports a reusable design pattern for auditable agent evaluation: define the state and risk dimensions, run a sufficiently long or complete workflow, gate outputs with an independent checker, and preserve failure traces for review. Confidence is medium overall because the primary records and available full HTML were inspected, but the corpus is dominated by recent preprints and no code, dataset, model, hardware, or clinical reproduction was performed.

## Detailed Summary

### Problem context

The source DEP groups research that moves beyond single-turn capability demonstrations. Its items examine failures that emerge over time, across subgroups, at workflow boundaries, or when generated outputs interact with an external system. The package therefore connects behavioral safety evaluation with medical robustness, scientific risk, embodied security, stateful tool use, formal proof, hardware optimization, and digital-twin integration.

### Long-horizon and population-aware evaluation

TSJ evaluates six model backbones across four developmental stages, 24 risk dimensions, and three psychological-vulnerability personas over 30-day simulated trajectories. The paper reports 432 independent trials and 12,960 simulated interaction days. Its AULC analysis separates risks that are visible early from risks that become more apparent through repeated interaction. The abstract reports stable risk estimation after about 140 turns; the HTML methods/results describe approximately 20 simulated days as a point after which additional exposure has diminishing marginal gain in the reported setting. These are related but not identical units, so this artifact does not convert one into the other.

BenchX builds a benchmark from 85,355 abdominal CT scans across six cohorts and attaches demographic and acquisition metadata such as age, sex, race, contrast phase, scanner type, voxel spacing, and tumor attributes. Its design treats subgroup and protocol performance as first-class evidence instead of relying on an aggregate average. The paper states that datasets, code, and models will be released; this availability claim was recorded but not independently checked.

### Workflow-aware and state-aware agent evaluation

AutoMedBench organizes autonomous medical-AI research into Plan, Setup, Validate, Inference, and Submit stages. It covers 24 tasks across five medical-imaging and multimodal tracks, with runs averaging 33 agent turns. The source reports that validation and submission errors account for 37.7% and 38.1% of fired error codes, while task-understanding errors account for 0.9%; a run with one fired error code has a 48% lower average overall score than a run with none. The reviewer interpretation is that operational control and evidence checking are distinct capabilities from understanding the research brief.

StateGen addresses tool-agent data scarcity with a four-role loop: user simulator, agent under test, state-grounded tool simulator, and multi-axis judge. A separate authoritative state manager maintains a structured world-state object and enforces a backend-is-truth invariant. The paper reports 64,698 evaluated conversations across three corpora, a 23-dimensional persona vector, and a tool-call hallucination score of 9.66/10. Its limitation section says the judge has not yet been calibrated against human gold-standard annotations, which constrains how absolute scores should be interpreted.

### Scientific search and executable verification

ASYS uses prior-guided agent proposals, evolutionary structural search, and gradient fitting for five PDE problems. The reported protocol scores candidates against PDE residuals, initial and boundary conditions, and compatibility constraints, then validates against independently generated numerical references hidden during search. The experiments ran on a single Apple M4 CPU with bounded wall-clock budgets. The paper explicitly positions ASYS as a structural characterizer for difficult individual evolutions rather than an amortized solver for many initial conditions.

LEAP combines informal reasoning, decomposition, iterative self-refinement, and Lean compiler interaction. Its Lean-IMO-Bench contains 60 manually formalized and verified problems split into Basic and Advanced sets. The paper reports solving all 12 Putnam 2025 problems in Lean and raising general-purpose model formal solve rates from below 10% to 70% on its benchmark, above a reported 48% specialized-system comparison. The checker makes proof validity more concrete than a language-only judge, but the result remains configuration- and benchmark-dependent.

AlphaEvolve adapts evolutionary code search to FHE kernels on Google Cloud TPUv5e. Candidate programs are filtered by compilation, randomized correctness checks, and security checks before latency rewards are returned to the search. The paper reports, within 24 hours of automated exploration, 2.5x TFHE bootstrap improvement and 1.31x/1.18x improvements for CKKS rotation/multiplication relative to the cited human-engineered baselines. The safety-relevant design point is the gate: optimization rewards are conditional on correctness and security, not raw speed alone.

### Risk, security, and physical-science integration

SciRisk-Bench evaluates AI4Science safety across seven disciplines, 31 subdisciplines, and ten explicit risk dimensions. Its results argue that discipline labels alone are insufficient because risk varies with the subdiscipline and unsafe mechanism. The source is useful as a taxonomy and diagnostic frame, but its benchmark scores were not independently recomputed.

The robotics SoK develops an F-E-S-G framework spanning foundation model, embodied system, supporting ecosystem, and governance impact. Its structured review begins with 290 candidates, retains 118 after full-text screening, and uses a final coded corpus of 96 papers. The cross-layer framing highlights a recurring gap between where a risk originates and where a mitigation is applied; a local monitor may suppress an unsafe action without removing an upstream poisoned model, planner, or external service.

The MPEX milestone report describes an AI Hot-Spot Controller, an E-beam Damage Assessment Digital Twin, and Galaxy workflows connecting selected physics codes to DOE HPC resources and planned data-acquisition integration. It reports 14,666 proto-MPEX discharges as an initial training-data source. This is evidence of workflow integration and milestone planning, not independent evidence that a production digital twin or autonomous controller is safe or validated.

### Cross-source conclusion

Across the ten records, the unit of trust shifts from a generated answer to a monitored process. The process needs a durable state model, a meaningful context partition, a checker or reviewer, and a trace that explains failures. The records differ in domain and maturity, so the pattern is a reviewer synthesis rather than a claim that all systems are equivalent.

## Potential Implementations

### 1. State-and-Stage Evaluation Ledger

- User: Agent evaluation engineers and research reviewers.
- Goal: Compare long-running agents without collapsing state or workflow failures into one score.
- Core mechanism: Store each event with scenario ID, state hash, stage, context slice, action, checker result, and reviewer disposition.
- Required inputs: Synthetic scenarios, tool schemas, safe model endpoints, stage definitions, and a versioned evaluator.
- Outputs: Trace ledger, per-stage scores, failure clusters, and public-safe review summary.
- Risk controls: Synthetic data by default, least-privilege tools, no raw secrets, retention limits, and human review for high-impact domains.
- Evaluation: Schema validation, replay consistency, held-out scenarios, and manual audit of a sample of traces.

### 2. Longitudinal Safety Simulator

- User: Safety researchers testing companion-like or persistent agents.
- Goal: Detect delayed relational or behavioral risks in authorized synthetic interaction.
- Core mechanism: Persona- and stage-conditioned scenarios with bounded state updates, trajectory metrics, and retrospective causal trace review.
- Required inputs: Synthetic personas, approved risk taxonomy, model endpoint, safety policy, and stop conditions.
- Outputs: Delayed-risk curves, dimension-level findings, and escalation recommendations.
- Risk controls: No real minors or vulnerable users, no therapeutic or clinical claims, synthetic data, safe sandboxing, and human safety review.
- Evaluation: Calibration against expert-labeled traces, sensitivity to session length, and false-positive/false-negative analysis.

### 3. Verifier-Gated Scientific Search Harness

- User: Authorized AI4Science or systems researchers.
- Goal: Explore candidate equations or kernels while rejecting invalid or unsafe outputs before reward.
- Core mechanism: Separate proposal, compilation/simulation, correctness/security checking, and scoring stages with immutable logs.
- Required inputs: Public equations or synthetic kernels, explicit constraints, test vectors, simulator, and resource budget.
- Outputs: Candidate lineage, checker outcomes, best valid candidate, and failure archive.
- Risk controls: Sandboxed execution, no production credentials, no unsafe physical actuation, resource caps, and domain-expert sign-off.
- Evaluation: Hold-out constraints, adversarial invalid candidates, baseline comparisons, and independent checker review.

## Three Ways to Exercise This Research

1. State-grounded toy agent: Objective: test whether explicit state reduces fabricated tool facts. Inputs: synthetic account records, a small tool schema, and a local model or scripted agent. Method: run matched state-grounded and prompt-only simulations with held-out scenarios. Output: trace ledger and hallucination/error comparison. Success criterion: fewer state-inconsistent tool results without lower task completion. Stop condition: stop on any real personal data, secret, or uncontrolled external side effect.
2. Workflow reliability replay: Objective: measure where an agent fails across Plan, Setup, Validate, Inference, and Submit. Inputs: a public or synthetic medical-imaging-shaped task with non-clinical toy data, stage contracts, and a checker. Method: run bounded attempts and label every fired error code. Output: stage-level scorecard and three highest-value workflow fixes. Success criterion: validation and submission failures are observable and reproducible. Stop condition: do not use patient data or make clinical decisions.
3. Constraint-gated symbolic search: Objective: evaluate whether a verifier prevents invalid candidate rewards. Inputs: a toy differential equation or safe optimization function, explicit constraints, a numerical reference, and a small compute budget. Method: compare search with and without hold-out constraint checks. Output: candidate lineage, valid/invalid counts, and reproducibility note. Success criterion: the gated loop never promotes a candidate that fails the independent checks. Stop condition: stop before connecting to production hardware or physical systems.

## Example MVP Product

- Product name: Evidence-Bound Agent Ledger
- Target user: Research and safety teams evaluating tool-using or workflow-oriented agents.
- Problem: Aggregate success scores hide where state drift, subgroup failure, validation omission, or checker mismatch occurred.
- Core workflow: Define a scenario and context slices; run a bounded agent workflow; capture state and actions; invoke independent checkers; cluster failures; export a public-safe report.
- Data requirements: Synthetic or authorized task scenarios, versioned tool schemas, structured state snapshots, stage contracts, checker outputs, and evaluator labels.
- Architecture: Local-first runner with a scenario registry, state store, event ledger, checker service, failure classifier, and Markdown/JSON report exporter. External model calls are optional and must be explicitly configured.
- Success metrics: Replay determinism, state-consistency rate, checker precision on seeded invalid cases, stage-level failure recall, time-to-review, and percentage of artifacts with complete provenance.
- Risk controls: No raw secrets or personal data in logs, sandboxed tools, allowlisted network access, resource limits, human review gates, retention policy, and safe-by-default synthetic fixtures.
- Limitations: The MVP cannot establish clinical safety, human developmental outcomes, physical-robot safety, or formal proof correctness beyond its configured checkers.
- MVP boundary: Evaluation and evidence capture only; no autonomous deployment or physical actuation.
- Deployment model: Local CLI or private batch runner with repository-exported Markdown.
- Evaluation plan: Unit-test the event schema, replay synthetic scenarios, inject state inconsistencies, compare checker outcomes, and conduct a human audit of exported reports.
- Failure modes: Incomplete traces, biased judges, stale tool schemas, false confidence from passing toy checks, and sensitive data leakage if input controls are bypassed.
- Maintenance plan: Version scenario packs, checkers, prompts, model identifiers, schemas, and retention rules; rerun a small regression suite whenever a dependency changes.
