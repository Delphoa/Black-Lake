## Executive Summary

The selected DEP is best understood not as ten unrelated technology updates but as a study of where a system places trust. Across agent security, data pipelines, scientific collaboration, distributed compute, edge inference, quantum simulation, and cellular senescence, the strongest designs externalize a boundary that can be inspected: an authorizer decides participation, a deterministic monitor decides authority, provenance resolves which records belong to a contributor, an oracle tests whether a failure existed, interventions test whether reasoning depends on its stated premise, routing preserves scientific context, schedulers expose hardware dependencies, Hamiltonian learning calibrates a physical simulator, and multi-omic signatures replace a single universal biological marker [E3-E14].

The most actionable reviewer interpretation is that trustworthy performance requires two coupled layers. The generative, analytic, or physical process produces candidate results; a separate evidence layer records identity, state, dependency, permission, calibration, or attribution. The sources support that pattern, but they do not establish one universal architecture. Their evaluators, domains, cost models, threat models, devices, and populations differ materially [E3-E14].

Evidence quality is strongest where the reviewed work exposes exact experimental conditions, artifacts, and caveats. It is weaker where results rely on one deployment, one model, one toy environment, one platform family, one biological campaign, or observational cohort associations. No code, dataset, model, benchmark, device, laboratory process, or cohort analysis was executed in this review. The synthesis is therefore suitable for architecture and evaluation planning, not a reproduction claim or clinical recommendation.

## Detailed Summary

### 1. Authority must be outside untrusted generation

The GDM roadmap treats an internal agent as a possible insider threat and maps control measures to four detection tiers and three prevention/response tiers. Its value is organizational: capability evidence should trigger stronger monitoring, access control, anomaly detection, and response infrastructure. Its authors also state that TRAIT&R is theoretical, mitigation placement is uncertain, and detection-heavy control may miss sandbagging or alignment-faking strategies [E4].

Composable Trust gives the pattern a narrower formal shape. A deterministic non-model monitor chooses the operation and any external action from trusted inputs, while an unmodified language model may still synthesize lower-trust information. The paper separates a structural authority-rejection property from measured prompt-injection resistance. On its held-out suite, genuine-leak defense increased from 27% to 94% with about a 4% clean-quality cost; under adaptive evaluation, the formal authority boundary held while the measured soft defense was reported at 87%. The proof does not convert the measured content defense into a universal guarantee [E6].

Agora shows the same tension in distributed training. Its training loop is peer-to-peer, but admission is not unconditional: a singleton Authorizer verifies packages, issues credentials, and provides a root of trust. The paper reports an 8.6B-parameter model trained on 500B FineWeb-Edu tokens over 40 days with 330 contributor nodes, about 170k tokens/s, 4.2 tokens per TFLOP, and 63% of a centralized H100 efficiency baseline. Continuous churn included 669 joins and 607 departures. These are substantial systems results, but they coexist with operator nodes, minimum hardware/network gates, and a centralized trust service [E3].

### 2. Provenance and intervention prevent false action

OriginBlame addresses the gap between an unlearning algorithm and the real-world task of identifying what to forget. It propagates contributor identity through processing, exposes record-level queries, and maintains a token index. On 219,555 Chinese Wikipedia pages, the paper reports reducing over-deletion from 101x to 1.3x, adding 1.3-4.0% throughput overhead in Hugging Face and 2.1-19.0% in Datatrove, and improving a reported NPO forgetting measure by 42% over same-size random forget sets on a 1.7B model. The system cannot retroactively recover lost provenance, currently has a narrow parser ecosystem, tracks direct mappings, and rebuilds indexes after data changes [E5].

Phantom Guardrails asks an earlier question: did the failure exist? In its deterministic Counterfactual Fabrication Lab, all presented episodes are legal and a byte-exact oracle can refute fabricated violations. A benign rule-shaped pattern produced a nonexistent-rule guard in 15 of 60 runs, compared with 0 of 60 for featureless legal inputs. A warrant-aware acceptance rule excluded the phantom across the reported loop while still adopting real fixes. This demonstrates a precise failure mode for suppression-only optimization, but only inside a controlled micro-lab [E7].

Interventional Grounding Audits asks whether a reasoning step changes when a stated premise is altered. On 50 ProntoQA problems with GPT-4o, consistent predicate substitution achieved F1 0.806 for proof-tree dependencies versus 0.343 for a self-consistency baseline; predicate-determining recall was 100%. The corrected artifact reports that 33 of 50 correctly solved problems had an aligned step insensitive to a direct structural dependency. The paper calls this a right-answer/wrong-reasoning signal, while also explaining that all such cases involved entity-introduction premises and that 10.5% of aligned certificates in the combined protocol were unparseable [E9].

Together, these studies define three preconditions for changing a system: identify the affected object, prove or test the asserted failure, and preserve an auditable link between evidence and action. A remediation without those preconditions can over-delete, overfit, or harden a fiction.

### 3. Shared context is a governed routing problem

Networked Intelligence presents Mycelium as an active context graph rather than a larger private prompt. The system captures observations and hypotheses, relates them to an evolving knowledge model, and routes them to another human or agent whose next decision can use them. The reported case is a microbial multi-omics campaign in which a local analysis became a cross-expert constraint and then an experimental design. This is a promising mechanism, not yet a multi-laboratory reliability result; the authors identify long-duration scale, conflict resolution, and cross-discipline translation as open problems [E8].

The governance implication is that context movement needs attribution, receiver selection, permission, freshness, and conflict handling. More context is not automatically better. A useful routing layer should explain why an item moved, which decision it may change, and who can authorize the downstream action.

### 4. Compute boundaries require calibrated scheduling

HeteroMosaic treats edge inference as a dependency and scheduling problem across CPU, iGPU, and NPU resources. It combines a heterogeneous roofline model, dependency-preserving micro-batches, and trace-guided co-optimization. On three AMD Ryzen AI platforms, the paper reports up to 1.73x over an iGPU baseline, 1.78x over an NPU baseline, 2.05x over frameworks such as llama.cpp on the balanced system, and up to 45.3% less energy. The paper also shows that decode gains can be near 1.0 on some model/platform pairs, numeric formats differ across frameworks, and platform-specific kernels, layouts, queues, and synchronization remain necessary [E10].

Agora and HeteroMosaic therefore share a boundary concept despite different scales: expose heterogeneity, measure the communication or memory constraint, and route work according to a calibrated control plane. Neither result supports the inference that adding devices alone improves efficiency.

### 5. Physical and biological claims need measurement-aware boundaries

The magnon study combines analog evolution with digital excitation on a superconducting processor of up to 97 qubits. Hamiltonian learning calibrates the evolution; the experiment then measures temperature-dependent spectra, lifetimes, self-scattering, and pump-probe interactions. The appendices report nontrivial fidelity loss and noise mitigation, and compare observables with exact small-system and tensor-network calculations. The result supports a calibrated quantum-simulation workflow, not an unqualified claim that classical simulation is obsolete [E11].

SenCat reaches a parallel conclusion in biology: no single marker universally identifies cellular senescence. It profiles transcriptomes and proteomes across 14 primary human cell types and more than 30 senescence paradigms, then derives machine-learning signatures that transfer to additional datasets. The paper highlights dynamic trajectories, culture-to-tissue transfer, and transcript-protein discrepancies as caveats [E12].

The circulating-signature study applies SenCat-derived proteins to 1,275 BLSA and 997 InCHIANTI participants. The reported signatures associate with age-related traits and health trajectories, with cross-cohort validation for many findings. The design is observational, uses one proteomic measurement per participant, and does not establish that changing a signature changes a health outcome [E13]. The lymph-node atlas adds spatial evidence: 99 sections from 51 donors and about 20 million cells show age-linked, cell-type-specific senescence patterns, including germinal-center B-cell localization. It remains preprint evidence and does not establish an intervention [E14].

Across the physical and biomedical sources, measurement is a model-dependent boundary. Calibration, sampling, preprocessing, and spatial or cell-type resolution decide what the resulting signal can support.

## Observations

- `Observed pattern`: The most credible systems separate a productive process from a verifier or control plane: model versus monitor, contributor versus Authorizer, data transformation versus provenance index, proposer versus oracle, reasoning trace versus intervention, accelerator kernels versus scheduler, analog evolution versus Hamiltonian learning, and biomarker abundance versus validated signature.
- `Observed pattern`: Negative evidence is unusually valuable. Agora reports admission floors and convergence thresholds; Phantom Guardrails supplies all-legal controls; Interventional Grounding Audits records unparseable cases and corrected results; HeteroMosaic shows near-1.0 decode cases; biomedical studies distinguish association from mechanism.
- `Technical implication`: A reusable boundary record needs identity, source integrity, state/version, allowed action, evidence trace, evaluator result, and revocation or stop semantics.
- `Contradiction or tension`: Decentralized or networked systems still rely on roots of trust, managed services, curated routing, or platform-specific runtimes. Distribution does not eliminate governance.
- `Contradiction or tension`: More observability can improve safety and science while increasing privacy, cost, centralization, and attack surface.
- `Open question`: Which boundary fields remain stable across model actions, training data, scientific context, hardware scheduling, and experimental measurement without collapsing important domain distinctions?
- `Reviewer hypothesis`: Warrant-aware acceptance is the common mechanism: an action proceeds only when evidence identifies the object, supports the proposed change, and remains valid under the relevant scope and version.

## Considerations

- A control plane becomes a high-value attack target and should use least privilege, signed state, separation of duties, independent monitoring, and recovery paths.
- Provenance can contain personal or contributor identifiers. Retention, access, deletion, consent, and license policy must be designed before broad collection.
- Chain-of-thought or trajectory monitoring can expose private data and may not reliably reveal latent intent. Logs should be minimized and access-controlled.
- Warrant-aware remediation should include a no-change outcome. An optimizer pressured to always improve may fabricate justification.
- Context routing needs conflict, freshness, retraction, privacy, and recipient-authorization semantics; otherwise an attributed graph can still propagate stale or harmful state.
- Cross-device scheduling benchmarks should normalize precision, quantization, thermal envelope, kernel maturity, batch shape, and measurement method.
- Quantum and biological measurements require calibration, error accounting, uncertainty intervals, and explicit boundaries on causal interpretation.
- Biomedical signatures are research tools, not diagnoses or treatment recommendations. Prospective validation and clinical governance are required before translation.

## Weaknesses

- Cross-domain synthesis risks treating metaphorical similarity as mechanistic equivalence. An authorization lattice, a data lineage index, a hardware scheduler, and a senescence signature are not interchangeable.
- Most source claims were not independently reproduced, and implementation availability is not reproducibility.
- GDM's roadmap and internal monitoring context are partly prospective and organization-specific [E4].
- Agora's permissionless framing coexists with a singleton Authorizer, operator nodes, and strict resource floors [E3].
- OriginBlame's main evaluation is concentrated in Wikipedia-derived processing and cannot restore missing historical provenance [E5].
- Composable Trust, Phantom Guardrails, and Interventional Grounding Audits use modest or synthetic evaluation suites [E6, E7, E9].
- Mycelium has one primary scientific campaign rather than multi-lab longitudinal evaluation [E8].
- HeteroMosaic depends on AMD-specific mechanisms, and some comparisons are not strict precision-matched tests [E10].
- The quantum experiment has material fidelity and noise constraints [E11].
- The biomedical evidence is heterogeneous and partly observational or preprint-based; it does not establish treatment effects [E12-E14].

## Potential Implementations

### 1. Boundary Receipt Service

- `User`: Agent-platform and workflow engineers.
- `Goal`: Record why a high-consequence tool action is allowed.
- `Core mechanism`: A deterministic policy service evaluates signed identity, source integrity, requested operation, evidence references, and current state before issuing a short-lived receipt.
- `Required inputs`: Synthetic tool request, policy version, evidence IDs, identity, and resource scope.
- `Outputs`: Allow, deny, or abstain decision plus a minimally disclosed receipt.
- `Risk controls`: Least privilege, expiry, revocation, replay prevention, independent audit, and redacted logs.
- `Evaluation`: Mutation tests, unauthorized-source injections, stale-state tests, and fail-safe drills.

### 2. Warrant-Aware Change Gate

- `User`: Maintainers of automated remediation or self-improving harnesses.
- `Goal`: Prevent changes that address nonexistent failures.
- `Core mechanism`: Require a reproducible failing fixture, oracle or invariant, minimal patch, and regression proof before accepting a generated change.
- `Required inputs`: Known-good and known-bad synthetic cases, proposed change, exact expected outcomes, and rollback plan.
- `Outputs`: Evidence bundle, acceptance decision, and rollback artifact.
- `Risk controls`: No-change option, bounded iterations, human review for irreversible changes, and protected baselines.
- `Evaluation`: Phantom-failure controls, real-failure controls, and adversarially misleading but legal inputs.

### 3. Provenance-Aware Forget-Set Builder

- `User`: Dataset stewards and privacy/compliance reviewers.
- `Goal`: Resolve a contributor request into the narrowest defensible training-data scope.
- `Core mechanism`: Propagate contributor and license metadata through a toy document pipeline, then produce record and token locators with uncertainty labels.
- `Required inputs`: Synthetic public-domain documents, contributor IDs, transformations, and retention policy.
- `Outputs`: Candidate forget set, over-deletion estimate, unresolved mappings, and audit report.
- `Risk controls`: Synthetic data, identifier minimization, encrypted indexes, access control, and deletion tests.
- `Evaluation`: Known-origin fixtures, edit/reconcile tests, and false-inclusion/false-exclusion measures.

### 4. Context Routing Auditor

- `User`: Research teams using human-agent workspaces.
- `Goal`: Explain when and why a scientific observation moved between contexts.
- `Core mechanism`: Score decision relevance, freshness, attribution, permission, and conflict before routing a claim.
- `Required inputs`: Synthetic research graph, role permissions, observations, hypotheses, and decision points.
- `Outputs`: Routed item, explanation, unresolved conflict, or abstention.
- `Risk controls`: Recipient consent, field-level privacy, retraction propagation, and no autonomous physical action.
- `Evaluation`: Missed-relevance, over-routing, stale-context, privacy, and conflict-resolution tests.

### 5. Calibrated Measurement Registry

- `User`: Systems and scientific-computing reviewers.
- `Goal`: Prevent benchmark or experimental claims from losing device, precision, calibration, or sampling context.
- `Core mechanism`: Store each measurement with method, version, hardware/sample boundary, preprocessing, uncertainty, and invalidation conditions.
- `Required inputs`: Synthetic benchmark traces or public aggregate measurements.
- `Outputs`: Comparable measurement cards and explicit non-comparability warnings.
- `Risk controls`: No participant-level data, provenance checks, immutable revisions, and withheld sensitive telemetry.
- `Evaluation`: Deliberately mismatched precision, device, cohort, and calibration fixtures.

## Three Ways to Exercise This Research

1. `Synthetic authority receipt`: Objective: test whether external authorization survives prompt injection. Inputs: a toy tool API, two source-integrity levels, and a fixed policy. Method: generate benign and injected requests, have the model propose actions, and let deterministic code issue or deny receipts. Output: decision matrix and replayable receipts. Success criterion: lower-trust text never changes the authorized operation or scope. Stop condition: any receipt exposes secrets, bypasses policy, or cannot be reproduced.
2. `Counterfactual remediation lab`: Objective: test whether an optimizer invents failures. Inputs: one known-good synthetic fixture, one seeded real failure, an exact oracle, and reversible candidate changes. Method: compare no-change, real-failure, and misleading-pattern conditions. Output: warrant bundle and accepted/rejected changes. Success criterion: real failures are fixed while known-good cases remain unchanged. Stop condition: the process proposes irreversible changes, cannot cite a reproducible failure, or exceeds its iteration budget.
3. `Measurement-context audit`: Objective: identify invalid cross-system comparisons. Inputs: public aggregate claims with deliberately varied precision, hardware, dataset/cohort, calibration, and version. Method: encode measurement cards and require a compatibility check before ranking results. Output: compatible groups, rejected comparisons, and missing-field list. Success criterion: every rejection names the incompatible boundary. Stop condition: a ranking proceeds with unresolved measurement or governance fields.

## Example MVP Product

- `Product name`: Boundary Ledger.
- `Target user`: Teams building agent workflows, data pipelines, and evaluation harnesses.
- `Problem`: Decisions are often recorded without the evidence, version, authority, or measurement context needed to review them later.
- `Core workflow`: Ingest a synthetic action or change request; attach source and state identifiers; run deterministic policy and warrant checks; issue allow, deny, or abstain; export a public-safe receipt and a private audit record.
- `Data requirements`: Synthetic requests, hashed source identifiers, policy versions, test fixtures, evaluator outputs, and no raw secrets or personal data.
- `Architecture`: Local-first CLI or small service with an append-only receipt store, policy engine, evidence adapter, redaction layer, and verification command. Generative models may suggest actions but cannot write receipts or change policy.
- `Success metrics`: Zero unauthorized operations in the test suite; 100% receipt replay; explicit abstention on missing critical fields; bounded false-denial rate on known-good actions; no secret or local-context leaks.
- `Risk controls`: Least privilege, short-lived receipts, signed policy versions, separation of proposer and authorizer, encrypted private logs, public-output sanitization, and manual approval for irreversible actions.
- `Limitations`: A correct receipt proves policy evaluation, not that the policy is complete, the evidence is truthful, or the downstream operation is safe.
- `MVP boundary`: Synthetic and authorized local tests only; no production credentials, offensive actions, clinical use, training-data deletion, or physical-device control.
- `Deployment model`: Local CLI with optional read-only dashboard.
- `Evaluation plan`: Unit tests for policy rules, mutation tests for evidence fields, replay tests, prompt-injection fixtures, phantom-failure fixtures, and privacy review.
- `Failure modes`: Stale policy, forged evidence reference, over-broad scope, evaluator drift, missing revocation, receipt replay, and redaction failure.
- `Maintenance plan`: Versioned policies and schemas, signed migration records, monthly fixture refresh, and independent review of high-impact rules.
