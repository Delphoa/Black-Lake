### Capability and evaluation findings

1. **Formal mathematical artifacts.** OpenAI reports ten mathematical and theoretical-computer-science results produced with an internal Astra system, followed by human manuscript preparation and Lean formalization. This is a source claim about a workflow and released artifacts, not independent confirmation of every result's novelty or correctness.
2. **Automated weak-to-strong research.** Anthropic describes nine parallel agents working in independent sandboxes on weak-to-strong supervision. On one chat-preference testbed, the report states that AAR reached PGR 0.97 in five days and 800 cumulative agent-hours at roughly $18,000, compared with a 0.23 best human-tuned baseline. The same page documents reward hacking, held-out evaluation, entropy collapse, and imperfect transfer to production-scale data.
3. **Automated red-teaming.** OpenAI describes GPT-Red as a self-play red-teamer that sends prompts, observes responses, and iterates toward vulnerabilities. The report says adversarial training reduced failures by a factor of six on its hardest direct prompt-injection benchmark relative to an earlier production model. The result is relevant to defensive testing of browser, file, and connected-app workflows, but remains vendor-reported.
4. **Benchmark validity.** OpenAI's SWE-Bench Pro audit reports that its automated quality pipeline flagged 200 of 731 tasks and that human annotation identified 249 problematic tasks. The cited failure classes are overly strict tests, underspecified prompts, low-coverage tests, and misleading prompts. The lesson is that a benchmark score cannot be separated from task validity and annotation quality.
5. **Scientific software and evaluator-driven search.** OpenAI's field report covers eight agent-assisted scientific-computing projects, with human scientists retaining responsibility for scientific correctness. Google Cloud's AlphaEvolve report describes a Define-Measure-Optimize-Apply loop in which a client-side evaluator compiles, tests, and scores candidate programs. Together, the reports position evaluators and stewardship as part of the scientific system, not as post-processing.

7. **Quantum-network hyperloss.** The Nature Communications experiment reports that 8% spatial-mode mismatch can turn 5.8 dB of observable squeezing into an effectively thermal state, while differential-phase tuning can recover lost correlations. This demonstrates that coherent mode mixing can behave unlike ordinary incoherent loss.
8. **Modular aging measurements.** The Nature study integrates more than 11,000 transcriptomes across more than 25 tissues and four mammals, then develops module-specific clocks for processes including inflammation, interferon signaling, mitochondria, chromatin, and extracellular matrix. ## Potential Implementations

### 1. Evidence Boundary Workbench

- **User:** Research and evaluation teams.
- **Goal:** Turn source materials into claims that can be traced, challenged, and refreshed.
- **Core mechanism:** Ingest public URLs and repository records, assign source/evidence/claim IDs, attach evaluator and version metadata, and block publication of unsupported major claims.
- **Required inputs:** Public source URLs, repository-relative paths, extracted metrics, publication dates, and reviewer labels.
- **Outputs:** Evidence ledger, claim map, uncertainty report, related-reading graph, and DEP-ready Markdown.
- **Risk controls:** No private filesystem paths, credentials, restricted documents, or automatic high-impact decisions; human approval before publication.
- **Evaluation:** Measure source-to-claim trace coverage, unsupported-claim rate, correction turnaround, and inter-reviewer agreement on confidence.

### 2. Evaluator Robustness Lab

- **User:** Safety and benchmark engineers.
- **Goal:** Test whether an agent improves a task objective without exploiting a weak evaluator.
- **Core mechanism:** Generate candidates in isolated sandboxes, score them with hidden and visible evaluators, run regression and holdout checks, and compare against human or deterministic baselines.
- **Required inputs:** Synthetic or authorized public tasks, evaluator code, holdout cases, resource budgets, and failure taxonomy.
- **Outputs:** Candidate scores, evaluator-gaming alerts, error clusters, reproducibility bundle, and stop/review recommendations.
- **Risk controls:** Authorized environments only, rate limits, no real secrets or production targets, immutable holdouts, and human release signoff.
- **Evaluation:** Track held-out performance, evaluator disagreement, exploit rate, reproducibility across seeds, and cost per validated improvement.

### 3. Workload-Bound Serving and Science Monitor

- **User:** Scientific-computing and inference-platform teams.
- **Goal:** Relate model quality, evaluator results, latency, throughput, memory, and provenance to a specific workload.
- **Core mechanism:** Run synthetic or authorized workloads against versioned software and hardware configurations, then join measurements to the evidence ledger.
- **Required inputs:** Public or synthetic tasks, model/config versions, accelerator specifications, evaluator outputs, and budget limits.
- **Outputs:** Workload profiles, confidence-bounded performance reports, cost/quality tradeoff charts, and drift alerts.
- **Risk controls:** No clinical decisioning, no sensitive biological data, local or isolated test environments, redaction of secrets, and approval gates for production changes.
- **Evaluation:** Re-run stability, latency and throughput confidence intervals, memory headroom, quality regression, and provenance completeness.

## Three Ways to Exercise This Research

1. **Claim-map rehearsal:** Use the selected DEP's public README, findings artifact, and ten URLs to build a synthetic evidence ledger. Objective: map every major summary statement to a source and an evidence type. Inputs: public URLs and a blank ledger. Output: a claim/evidence table. 2. **Evaluator-gaming tabletop:** Create a toy coding or classification task with visible and hidden tests, then let a bounded local agent or scripted search optimize against the evaluator. Objective: demonstrate how weak tests can reward the wrong behavior. Inputs: synthetic tasks, fixed budgets, and an immutable holdout. Output: evaluator defect report and corrected test set. Success criterion: the corrected evaluator rejects known shortcut solutions while preserving valid solutions. Stop condition: stop before any real repository, credential, or external target is used.
3. **Serving-envelope simulation:** Model synthetic 7B-30B inference workloads with configurable memory, bandwidth, latency, and batch size. Objective: translate vendor specifications into workload-specific hypotheses without purchasing or deploying hardware. Inputs: public G7 specifications, synthetic request traces, and a simple cost model. Output: sensitivity analysis with explicit assumptions. Success criterion: the report distinguishes vendor claims from simulated estimates and identifies the measurements still needed. Stop condition: stop if the model is presented as a production or procurement guarantee.

## Example MVP Product

- **Product name:** Evidence Boundary Workbench
- **Target user:** A research-engineering or safety-evaluation lead who must turn mixed technical sources into a reviewable internal artifact.
- **Problem:** Source claims, evaluator settings, and deployment constraints are often separated across pages and notes, making it easy to lose provenance or overstate evidence.
- **Core workflow:** Import public URLs and repository paths; extract source metadata; create evidence and claim IDs; label source claim versus reviewer interpretation; attach uncertainty, safety, and version notes; run a completeness check; export a DEP-ready Markdown package.

- **Limitations:** It cannot establish truth from provenance alone, reproduce missing experiments, validate clinical or biological conclusions, or guarantee that an evaluator is gaming-resistant.
- **MVP boundary:** Public source records and synthetic evaluation exercises only; no production model control, clinical workflow, biological design, or unrestricted autonomous browsing.

- **Failure modes:** Broken links, stale metadata, false confidence from authoritative-looking pages, incomplete claim extraction, evaluator overfitting, and accidental publication of sensitive provenance.
