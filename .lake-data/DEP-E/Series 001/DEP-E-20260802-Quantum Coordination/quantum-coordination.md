## Executive Summary

The selected DEP previously synthesized ten papers as an evidence-systems problem: capable agents and scientific systems still fail when they select the wrong evidence, recover without a budget, trust laundered authority, or collapse a multi-stage process into one endpoint score. This iterative pass deepens that synthesis through a randomly selected primary review, *Quantum Synchronization*, and four experimental anchors.

The expansion changes the coordination claim from metaphor to a measurable design discipline. The review does not offer one universal synchronization score. It organizes temporal correlations, quantum correlations, phase and frequency locking, Liouvillian spectra, and time-resolved emissions; distinguishes entrainment from mutual synchronization; separates transient from stable behavior; and shows that few-body and many-body systems require different mechanisms and observables. The trapped-ion experiment adds a particularly useful warning for agent and evidence systems: a synchronized relative phase can be inaccessible to local measurements and visible only through joint readout.

Reviewer interpretation: trustworthy coordination should be evaluated through a vector of state, timing, provenance, authority, cost, and joint-outcome measures. This is a cross-domain implementation hypothesis, not a claim made by the quantum sources. Confidence is high in the source taxonomy and medium in the transfer to agent systems because no code, benchmark, simulator, dataset, or physical experiment was reproduced.

## Detailed Summary

### Continuity from the selected DEP

The original source package joins ten research threads. GEAR treats long-context failure as indiscriminate copying rather than simply insufficient context length. CodeRescue treats post-failure action as a cost-calibrated routing decision. *They'll Verify* shows that multiple model roles can normalize malicious intent when authority claims enter upstream. PathAgentBench separates region acquisition from reasoning over curated evidence. SciCodePile requires executable tests rather than surface similarity. SkewAdam changes which optimizer states receive scarce memory. ATLAS amortizes an expensive physical sampling problem. PhoenixRepair expands repair-location and strategy search. The safety perspective moves failure analysis across epistemic, control, temporal, organizational, and ecosystem layers. The prior synthesis summarized these as provenance-bearing evidence objects and bounded recovery.

### New expansion: quantum synchronization

The selected review defines synchronization as coordinated rhythms in open quantum systems, then builds from classical nonlinear oscillators to quantum master equations, stochastic trajectories, mean-field phase reduction, finite-level systems, many-body phases, thermodynamics, and applications. Its central methodological lesson is pluralism: synchronization can be tested with temporal correlations of observables, quantum-information correlations, phase-space localization, frequency locking, spectral structure in the Liouvillian, or time-resolved emissions. These measures answer different questions and may disagree.

In few-body systems, the review covers quantum van der Pol oscillators, spin systems, entrainment to external fields, mutual synchronization, quantum synchronization blockade, transient synchronization, and trajectory-level behavior. Experimental platforms include digital quantum simulation, laser-cooled atoms, nuclear spins, optomechanics, trapped ions, and superconducting devices. The newly inspected IBM-Q and spin-1 atomic records anchor the review's account of engineered dissipation, phase localization, blockade, and Arnold-tongue-like response regions.

Many-body synchronization introduces collective phase transitions, scaling behavior, metastable oscillations, and continuous time crystals. The review notes that quantum noise can raise the critical coupling required for synchronization and that finite-system averages can obscure oscillatory single realizations. The continuous-time-crystal record provides a primary experimental anchor: a continuously pumped dissipative atom-cavity system exhibited persistent intracavity-photon oscillations with run-dependent phase and robustness to temporal perturbations.

The newest trapped-ion anchor demonstrates mutual synchronization between two quantum van der Pol oscillators. Its reported synchronized state is a stable relative phase available through joint readout even though individual oscillator phases do not expose it. This is directly useful to the review synthesis: local component checks can pass while a collective state remains unmeasured, and conversely a global endpoint can look stable while its local evidence is incomplete.

### Thermodynamics and application boundary

The review links synchronization to work cost, nonequilibrium fluctuation bounds, quantum heat engines, sensing, communication, memories, timekeeping, and distributed processing. It also states that the thermodynamics remains underdeveloped and that concrete demonstrations of improved technology performance are a major open challenge. Therefore this artifact treats applications as research directions unless the inspected source provides a direct experiment. Synchronization is not assumed to be free, beneficial, or uniquely quantum merely because phase locking is observed.

### Cross-domain synthesis

The continuity set and quantum expansion converge on a coordination ledger. A system should record: which local states were observed; which joint state was inferred; what clock or version aligned the evidence; which source or actor had authority; how much work or compute was spent; what measure defined success; and which perturbations or distribution shifts were tested. The proposed transfer is reviewer inference. Its value is falsifiability: each field can reject or refine the ledger using its own measurements instead of accepting a loose analogy.

## Potential Implementations

### 1. Multi-agent coordination ledger

- `User`: Agent-platform and safety-evaluation teams.
- `Goal`: Detect disagreement or hidden coupling across role-local outputs.
- `Core mechanism`: Record each role's observed state, evidence, authority, clock, and action, then compute bounded joint-readout checks.
- `Required inputs`: Synthetic task traces, policy versions, evidence IDs, timestamps, action costs, and reviewer labels.
- `Outputs`: Local-state table, joint-state report, unresolved conflicts, and stop reason.
- `Risk controls`: No production credentials, no autonomous merge authority, immutable inputs, and human review for consequential actions.
- `Evaluation`: Detection recall for seeded cross-role failures, false-positive rate, review time, and cost.

### 2. Evidence-acquisition synchrony evaluator

- `User`: Benchmark designers in scientific code, public imaging, or research retrieval.
- `Goal`: Determine whether components coordinate on the same valid evidence rather than merely agree on an answer.
- `Core mechanism`: Compare evidence IDs, acquisition times, validation results, and final claims across agents or pipeline stages.
- `Required inputs`: Public toy evidence graphs, support labels, deterministic validators, and bounded traces.
- `Outputs`: Evidence-overlap vector, stale-source alerts, acquisition failures, and endpoint result.
- `Risk controls`: Public or synthetic data only, prompt-injection isolation, and no clinical interpretation.
- `Evaluation`: Support recall, distractor rate, version consistency, endpoint accuracy, and calibration.

### 3. Recovery phase-map harness

- `User`: Maintainers of authorized coding agents.
- `Goal`: Map when local retry, alternative search, escalation, or stopping produces stable recovery.
- `Core mechanism`: Run seeded failures across explicit cost and perturbation settings, analogous to a bounded phase diagram without importing physical claims.
- `Required inputs`: Toy repositories, deterministic tests, repair actions, costs, seeds, and stop limits.
- `Outputs`: Action frontier, failure trajectories, regression checks, and stability regions.
- `Risk controls`: Sandboxed execution, allowlisted commands, no network credentials, and review-only patches.
- `Evaluation`: Solve rate, tail cost, regressions, stability under shift, and stop-rule compliance.

### 4. Quantum-review evidence map

- `User`: Quantum-technology literature reviewers.
- `Goal`: Keep measures, platforms, system scale, and application claims traceable.
- `Core mechanism`: Encode each paper by system, coupling, dissipation, observable, time regime, measure, experiment type, and claimed application.
- `Required inputs`: Public papers and canonical metadata.
- `Outputs`: Comparison matrix, missing-evidence flags, and replication backlog.
- `Risk controls`: No hardware control, no unpublished data, and explicit distinction between proposal, simulation, and experiment.
- `Evaluation`: Citation accuracy, reviewer agreement, source coverage, and correction rate.

## Three Ways to Exercise This Research

1. **Synthetic joint-readout test**: Objective: reveal a failure invisible to role-local checks. Inputs: three synthetic agent traces with one seeded cross-role inconsistency. Method: compare local pass/fail results with a joint evidence-and-authority check. Output: discrepancy report. Success criterion: the joint check identifies the seeded inconsistency without adding false authority. Stop condition: no production data, credentials, or external writes.
2. **Coordination-vector benchmark**: Objective: test whether a vector beats one endpoint score for review decisions. Inputs: public toy tasks with evidence, timing, cost, and outcome labels. Method: compare reviewer calibration using endpoint-only versus vector reports. Output: decision-quality and review-time results. Success criterion: improved error detection at bounded review cost. Stop condition: stop if private or consequential data enters the task.
3. **Quantum evidence-map exercise**: Objective: reproduce the selected review's classification at literature level, not physical level. Inputs: the five public quantum records labeled new in this pass. Method: code system scale, synchronization type, observable, experiment status, and limitation. Output: a small source-traceable matrix. Success criterion: every field resolves to a canonical source and proposals remain distinct from experiments. Stop condition: no hardware operation or unsupported numerical inference.
