## Insights and Relevance

Across otherwise different domains, the evidence converges on a common design requirement: evaluate and govern the complete trajectory. Codex usage data shows work shifting toward longer, parallel, reusable delegation; scBench-Long shows that the model–harness pair changes scientific outcomes; ToolPrivacyBench shows that successful completion can coexist with unauthorized intermediate disclosure; and HORIZON shows both the power and reward-hacking risk of iterative executable feedback. The remaining papers demonstrate that structured laws, identifiable regimes, explicit graph constraints, hardware-aware execution, and scalable approximations can make complex systems more observable or tractable. For downstream Black Lake work, the durable relationship is between autonomy and verification: increasing execution scope must be paired with purpose-bound data views, independent acceptance gates, versioned traces, and domain-specific evidence.
