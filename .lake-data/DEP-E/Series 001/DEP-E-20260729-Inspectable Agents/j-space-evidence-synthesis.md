# J-Space Evidence Synthesis

## Synthesis Question

How do the method, causal experiments, structural observations, alignment cases, and training intervention combine into one bounded evidentiary argument?

## Causal Argument Map

1. **Transport:** An averaged downstream Jacobian maps intermediate state into later vocabulary coordinates.
2. **Sparse interface:** A small nonnegative set of token directions reconstructs the observed J-space component.
3. **Reportability:** Concept swaps and injections alter later reports.
4. **Mediation:** Component swaps and clamping show that some non-J-space effects operate by re-entering J-space.
5. **Intermediate use:** Two-hop and arithmetic interventions alter downstream answers in computation order.
6. **Flexible reuse:** One argument representation can feed several downstream functions, with incomplete success.
7. **Selectivity:** Several routine tasks survive substantial disruption while flexible tasks degrade.
8. **Workspace structure:** Capacity, MLP amplification, and attention relay support a limited broadcast interface.
9. **Audit application:** Selected silent strategic representations can be read and causally tested.
10. **Training application:** Reflection training changes both readouts and behavior, with partial ablation reversal.

The chain is strongest through step 5. Steps 6-8 support a global-workspace analogy but depend on selected tasks and lens-derived boundaries. Steps 9-10 establish possibility for alignment research, not calibrated deployment performance.

## Evidence Convergence

| Evidence family | What it rules out | What it does not rule out |
|---|---|---|
| Readout rankings | Complete absence of token-aligned signal | Correlation, epiphenomenal decoding, or missed distributed concepts |
| Swaps and injections | Purely passive observation | Off-manifold or oversized intervention artifacts |
| Decomposition and clamping | Simple “all variance matters equally” account | Alternative sparse routes not represented by selected tokens |
| Task ablations | Universal dependence on J-space | Dependence outside the tested task battery |
| Broadcast-head ablations | Completely uniform propagation | Other relay mechanisms and architecture-specific effects |
| Training plus direction ablation | Behavior-only change unrelated to observed directions | Other simultaneous post-training mechanisms |

## Integrated Interpretation

The combined evidence supports a small causal interface that reformats some latent information into a reusable, vocabulary-aligned form. Report, explicit reasoning, and novel composition appear more likely to load this interface than routine processing. The interface is neither the whole residual stream nor a guaranteed bottleneck.

This resolves an apparent tension in the source: J-space explains little activation variance yet can dominate selected intervention outcomes. A narrow control surface can be functionally important because downstream modules preferentially amplify and relay it.

For alignment work, the same property is both useful and dangerous. Token alignment makes silent content inspectable, but it also makes incomplete signals feel definitive. The source's bypass evidence requires a multi-signal policy: behavior and outcome receipts remain primary; J-space can prioritize questions and causal tests.

## Claim Status Ledger

| Status | Claim |
|---|---|
| Supported in tested settings | Sparse token-aligned components mediate selected report and flexible-computation effects |
| Supported with boundary | Intermediate capacity and broadcast resemble functional workspace properties |
| Demonstrated as possibility | Selected alignment-relevant representations can be exposed and intervened upon |
| Promising but unreplicated | Counterfactual reflection can reshape workspace content and behavior |
| Not established | Complete access to cognition, universal monitoring, proof of intent, or subjective consciousness |

## Resulting Research Position

Treat J-space as a hypothesis-generating and intervention-guiding surface. A defensible claim should include:

- the exact model and lens build;
- the behavioral event;
- the readout and context;
- a matched control;
- a causal intervention when feasible;
- uncertainty and no-finding semantics;
- a statement of what bypass routes remain.
