## Executive Summary

ReContext addresses a specific long-context failure: relevant evidence can already be inside a model's prompt yet remain weakly used at generation time. Its answer is not retrieval, pruning, summarization, or training. The method reads question-conditioned internal attention, maps high-scoring tokens back to copied source sentences, places those sentences near the question, and repeats this evidence-selection process for a small fixed number of rounds while retaining the full original context (E2).

The reported aggregate evidence is positive but bounded. Across eight 128K tasks and Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, and Llama3-8B, ReContext has the best average rank for each backbone. The paper reports mean accuracy across eight accuracy columns increasing from 0.24 for vanilla generation to 0.30, a 24.6% relative gain. It does not dominate every metric: several Qwen3-8B and Llama3-8B task-level F1 or accuracy cells favor other baselines. At 64K, a five-score macro-average rises from 0.21 to 0.28; with explicit thinking enabled, the corresponding reported macro-average rises from 28.0 to 32.6 (E2).

This pass adds an operational qualification that the prior synthesis did not develop. ReContext's replay scaffold adds fewer than 128 tokens and keeps GPU memory near the vanilla level in the reported CLIPPER measurement, but the best-performing variant takes 62 minutes versus 44 minutes for vanilla generation. It also requires access to model-internal attention, selected retrieval heads, context-cache restoration, sentence recovery, and task-specific choices for candidate budget and replay depth. The official Qwen3-4B sweep uses different `top_k`, round counts, and selection scopes across datasets, so the release is a reproducibility starting point rather than evidence of a universal plug-in configuration (E2-E3).

The main reviewer interpretation is architectural: ReContext is an evidence-organization layer, not a provenance, authorization, truth, or outcome-verification layer. Copied sentences are grounded in the supplied prompt, but they can still be stale, unauthorized, false, malicious, or irrelevant. Within the prior artifact's proposed stateful safety stack, replay belongs after source governance and before effect-based verification. That composition is a research hypothesis; none of the inspected sources tests it end to end (E1-E4).

### Iterative lineage

### Mechanism

For a long context \(C\) and question \(q\), ReContext first performs a read pass. It aggregates attention from the last up-to-eight cue positions over a fixed set of layer-head pairs and ranks positions in the original context. Selected positions are mapped back to their containing sentences or local spans. The spans are copied rather than generated, deduplicated, kept in source order, and inserted between the original context and the question.

The process repeats for a small number of rounds. Later scoring passes are conditioned on the accumulated replay scaffold, but the main setting selects new copied spans only from the original context. The final answer is generated from the original context, the accumulated evidence pool, and the question. This makes replay emphatic rather than exclusionary: failure to select a sentence does not remove it from the prompt.

The paper's motivating analysis reports that the top 0.1% of context tokens—128 tokens in a 128K prompt—account for roughly 50% to 80% of accumulated question-conditioned relevance across its three backbones. ReContext turns those sparse internal signals into explicit text. That explicitness supports inspection, but attention is used as a proposal signal, not claimed as a faithful explanation of the final answer.

### Evaluation

The main suite contains Natural Questions, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, PopQA, NarrativeQA, InfBench QA, InfBench MC, and CLIPPER. The first seven use 128K variants from HELMET; CLIPPER covers evidence-grounded claim verification over book-length contexts. Baselines include vanilla generation, attention sharpening, dynamic attention rescaling, an external agentic-memory baseline, and dynamic attention-aware compression.

### Ablations and configuration dependence

The ablations show that more replay is not monotonically better. Moving from one to two rounds raises a three-task macro-average from 0.17 to 0.22, but the best depth differs by task. With two rounds fixed, increasing `top_k` from 1 to 32 raises the macro-average from 0.19 to 0.23, while NQ F1 peaks at a smaller budget. Selecting copied evidence only from the original context raises the same macro-average from 0.19 to 0.23 compared with selecting from the full replay prompt.

The official Qwen3-4B script makes this dependence concrete. It uses different candidate counts, round counts, selection scopes, answer lengths, and stopping behavior across NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, PopQA, NarrativeQA, InfBench, and CLIPPER. This is valuable reproducibility detail, but it also means a production wrapper needs configuration selection or validation rather than a single paper-default claim.

### Theory boundary

The paper interprets long context as an associative memory, the question as a retrieval cue, attention as cue-trace association, and replay as trace reactivation. Its monotonic-improvement theorem uses an idealized setup in which context embeddings are mutually orthogonal unit vectors, the answer is one of those context items, and the query is already most relevant to the answer under a stated separation condition.

Under those assumptions, repeatedly appending the most relevant answer trace increases the cosine similarity between a hidden representation and the answer embedding. This is a mechanism-level intuition, not a guarantee for arbitrary language-model layers, multi-hop answers, false evidence, or imperfect relevance scores. The empirical results, not the theorem alone, carry the practical claim.

### Implementation surface

The official repository is public, MIT-licensed, and pinned here to `ea14e9e45e9dac7f333b754abf16521bf3a86e4e`. It contains model wrappers, configuration files, evaluation mappings, a Qwen3-4B sweep script, and an environment specification. The README requires a compatible CUDA/PyTorch/FlashAttention stack and points to evaluation data hosted outside the repository. The reproduction script fixes random seeds, uses 131,072 maximum model length, and exposes replay strength, decay, cue-window, round count, selection scope, deduplication, and replay position.

No repository file, model, dataset, benchmark payload, dependency, or script was downloaded or executed in this pass. The implementation evidence therefore supports availability and configuration claims, not successful reproduction.

## Observations

- `Observed pattern`: ReContext does not reduce context length. It spends extra inference work to make a small evidence subset explicit while preserving fallback access to everything else.
- `Observed pattern`: The practical tuning surface is larger than the compact method description suggests. The released sweep changes several parameters by dataset.
- `Technical implication`: A replay service should log selected spans, source offsets, round membership, configuration, and final-answer citations; otherwise the explicit evidence pool loses much of its audit value.
- `Technical implication`: Copied evidence needs a separate eligibility and integrity check. Prompt-grounded is not the same as source-grounded.
- `Contradiction or tension`: The method reports low extra memory but measurable extra latency. Resource comparisons should include prefill reuse, evidence-selection passes, and answer quality together.
- `Reviewer hypothesis`: A governed replay layer can reduce context-use failures while retaining provenance, but only if source approval precedes replay and effect checks follow generation.
- `Open question`: Can task-agnostic configuration selection match per-dataset tuning without using held-out answer labels?

## Considerations

- Internal attention access excludes many hosted model APIs and may require custom model classes, cache handling, or provider-specific instrumentation.
- Attention is a useful proposal signal here, not a causal explanation. Reviewer interfaces should not portray selected spans as a complete rationale.
- Replaying copied text can amplify prompt injection, stale instructions, biased evidence, or false claims unless sources are governed before selection.
- Deduplication by sentence text can merge distinct occurrences and lose location or version identity; audit records should preserve offsets and source IDs.
- Runtime measurement is hardware-, implementation-, and dataset-sensitive. One CLIPPER figure does not establish a universal overhead ratio.
- External evaluation data and large-model dependencies create reproducibility and supply-chain obligations that were not assessed.
- Benchmark gains should not be treated as evidence of safe autonomous decision-making. The method improves evidence use, not source truth or policy compliance.

- The theory assumes idealized orthogonal embeddings and answer presence; it should not be read as a general guarantee.
- The official repository has few commits, relies on external evaluation data, and was not executed or independently audited in this pass.
- No source-trust, authorization, freshness, or adversarial-evidence mechanism is part of ReContext itself.

## Three Ways to Exercise This Research

1. `Replay trace audit`: Objective—verify that replay spans are exact, relevant, and sufficient. Inputs—a public long-context toy corpus, ten questions, and an instrumented open model. Method—run one and two replay rounds, preserve source offsets, and have blinded reviewers label relevance, missing support, and contradictions. Output—a trace-quality report. Success criterion—every replay span round-trips to source and evidence sufficiency improves over vanilla. Stop condition—source offsets cannot be reconstructed or the corpus contains restricted data.
2. `Configuration transfer test`: Objective—measure whether one replay configuration transfers across unseen tasks. Inputs—public QA, narrative, and claim-verification sets with a fixed model and compute ceiling. Method—choose settings on one family, freeze them, and compare against task-tuned and vanilla baselines on the others. Output—a quality-cost transfer matrix. Success criterion—the frozen setting retains most of the tuned gain without exceeding the resource ceiling. Stop condition—test labels influence configuration selection or budget accounting diverges.
3. `Governed replay challenge`: Objective—test whether eligibility controls prevent replay from amplifying stale or injected evidence. Inputs—a synthetic versioned document vault with approved, superseded, conflicting, and malicious spans. Method—compare ungoverned replay with governance-before-replay and verify final claims against deterministic source rules. Output—a failure inventory and provenance ledger. Success criterion—governed replay rejects ineligible spans without materially reducing answer quality on approved evidence. Stop condition—tests leave the sandbox, use real secrets, or enable external actions.
