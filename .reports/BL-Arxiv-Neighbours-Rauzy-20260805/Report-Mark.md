## Concise Research Notes

### Problem

Self-affine tiles and Rauzy fractals can induce tilings or multi-tilings whose overlap structure is represented by graphs. A contact graph is generally easier and smaller to construct, but the neighbor graph contains the fuller overlap information needed for tiling and boundary analysis. The paper asks whether the neighbor graph can be recovered algorithmically from the contact graph in both settings.

### Method

For self-affine tiles, the paper revisits a contact-to-neighbor construction based on repeated graph coronas and reduction. For Rauzy fractals generated by Pisot substitutions, it defines prefix-suffix graphs, subtiles, self-replicating translation sets, self-replicating boundary/contact graphs, and simpler graph variants. Algorithm 2 starts with the simple contact graph, forms a C-corona, reduces to the largest subgraph whose nodes lie on walks ending in loops, and repeats until the graph stabilizes.

### Evidence and results

Theorem 4.6 states that Algorithm 2 terminates after finitely many steps and outputs the self-replicating neighbor graph of a Pisot substitution. The proof uses finite connectivity by ±C-connections, a uniform bound on contact degree, monotonicity under the dual substitution, and a decomposition of higher-contact-degree walks into lower-degree and degree-one walks. The examples use two explicit substitutions. For σ1, the paper reports a self-replicating contact graph with 14 vertices and a simple contact neighbor graph with 26 vertices; the resulting simple neighbor graph is reported to coincide with the simple contact graph. For σ2, the displayed neighbor graph is obtained from a 15-vertex self-replicating contact graph.

### Limitations

The work is a mathematical construction rather than an empirical benchmark. It does not provide runtime scaling, memory measurements, a released implementation, or a broad stress test over substitution families. The proof imports prior geometric and graph results, and the practical cost of enumerating ambient graph nodes and repeated reductions is not quantified. The examples demonstrate the mechanism but do not establish deployment-level performance.

### Implementation relevance

The transferable engineering pattern is an invariant-preserving fixed-point computation: begin with a compact observable structure, expand only through a bounded local relation, reduce by a correctness predicate, and stop at a stable graph. Any implementation should expose node counts, edge counts, reduction reasons, cycle reachability, contact degree, and a reproducible stopping certificate.

### Reviewer interpretation

The paper's strongest contribution is not merely a graph algorithm; it is the proof that a local expansion operator recovers a global overlap object under a finite structural bound. This suggests a general design pattern for auditable reconstruction from compressed representations. The analogy to the related DEP entries is conceptual only: Moran Spectra controls asymptotic density in self-similar frequency sets, Flag Hardy Operators preserves estimates across geometric scales, and Acoustic Phase Retrieval reconstructs hidden fields from deliberately improved observability.

## Synthesis Note

### Concept Bridge

The selected paper turns a compact contact representation into a fuller neighbor representation through an iterative, locally bounded graph expansion and a global reduction predicate. Moran Spectra contributes the idea that a coarse asymptotic invariant can be controlled separately from a sparse structural correction. Flag Hardy Operators contributes a proof strategy that respects the geometry of multiple scales instead of flattening them into one generic norm. Acoustic Phase Retrieval contributes the operational rule that reconstruction should be conditioned on observability and a stability margin. Together, the four artifacts suggest an auditable pipeline: identify a compact invariant-bearing representation, expand through a constrained relation, certify a fixed point or stability margin, and publish both the result and the evidence ledger.

### Potential Implementations

1. Contact-to-neighbor graph inspector. Accept a finite contact graph and a bounded corona rule, iterate reduction, and emit each graph version, node/edge counts, cycle-reachability status, and a termination certificate. Use synthetic substitutions only; do not claim a tiling theorem for unsupported inputs.
2. Multiscale proof-audit workbench. Represent scale levels, local transitions, decomposition rules, and imported lemmas as a dependency graph. Run static checks for missing assumptions, unbounded contact degree, and reductions that lose required loop reachability.
3. Fractal-spectrum reconstruction sandbox. Generate small Moran-like and substitution-derived toy structures, compare graph fixed points with Fourier or spectral diagnostics, and gate interpretation on condition margins and finite-size warnings. Treat numerical agreement as an educational diagnostic, not as proof.

### Deeper Relationship Observations

1. All four artifacts separate a compact local representation from a global property: graph overlap completeness, spectral density, operator boundedness, or inverse-source recoverability.
2. The decisive resource is a finite or controlled margin: bounded contact degree, scale-decay exponents, asymptotic dimension, or a determinant lower bound. Without that margin, expansion or reconstruction can become non-auditable.
3. Proof-oriented mathematics and engineering instrumentation meet at the fixed-point boundary. A useful system should expose the invariant that makes termination, stability, or completeness plausible rather than returning only a final object.

### Conceptual Similarities

1. Geometry-aware decomposition: contact graphs, flag scales, self-similar digits, and Fourier modes preserve structure that generic flattening would discard.
2. Iterative refinement with a stopping condition: reduced coronas, multiscale recombination, tree-map construction, and Fourier reconstruction all require explicit termination or error criteria.
3. Evidence before interpretation: each artifact distinguishes structural assumptions from observed output and warns that finite examples do not certify asymptotic or infinite-dimensional claims.

### MVP Implementations with Code Mock-Ups

1. Fixed-point graph reducer.

~~~python
def reduce_to_fixed_point(seed, expand, retain_cycle_reachable):
    current = set(seed)
    history = [frozenset(current)]
    while True:
        candidate = set(expand(current))
        next_graph = set(retain_cycle_reachable(candidate))
        if next_graph == current:
            return {"graph": next_graph, "iterations": len(history), "stable": True}
        current = next_graph
        history.append(frozenset(current))
~~~

2. Evidence-ledger gate.

~~~python
def evidence_gate(node_count, edge_count, contact_degree, stable, sources):
    return {
        "stable": bool(stable),
        "finite_graph": node_count >= 0 and edge_count >= 0,
        "bounded_contact_degree": contact_degree >= 0,
        "source_count": len(sources),
        "reviewable": bool(stable and sources and contact_degree >= 0),
    }
~~~

def reconstruction_diagnostic(graph_margin, condition_margin, residual):
    return {
        "graph_margin_ok": graph_margin > 0,
        "condition_margin_ok": condition_margin > 0,
        "residual": float(residual),
        "interpretation_allowed": graph_margin > 0 and condition_margin > 0,
    }

### Developer Challenges

1. Implement the C-corona and reduction operators with deterministic ordering, bounded memory, and a machine-readable reason for every retained or removed node.
2. Add property-based tests for fixed-point stability, symmetry, loop reachability, and failure on deliberately unbounded or malformed synthetic inputs.
3. Build a cross-domain evidence schema that records structural assumptions, invariant margins, source versions, and stop conditions without implying mathematical proof from a finite run.

### Author Challenges

1. Publish a reference implementation or executable graph tables for the two examples so termination and vertex counts can be independently reproduced.
2. Quantify how the number of ambient nodes, corona expansions, and reductions scale across broader Pisot-substitution families.
3. State and test the boundary of the method for more general graph-directed iterated-function systems where the extra Rauzy structure may be absent.
